Quick buying tips

  • Do the wobble test. In-store or on arrival: press opposite corners of the table. Any rock means poor joinery — send it back.
  • Sit for 10 minutes. Chair comfort reveals itself slowly. Shallow seats and hard backs become miserable by dessert.
  • Measure the chairs-out footprint. Tables are measured without chairs; add 75–90 cm all round for pushed-back chairs.
  • Check the extension mechanism. On extending tables, read buyer reviews specifically about the mechanism — it's the number one failure point.
  • Think about cleaning. Glass and marble-effect show everything; wood hides a multitude of sins.

Dining Set FAQs

What size dining table do I need?

Allow 60 cm of table width per person and 75–90 cm of pull-out space behind each chair. A 120 cm table seats four; a 180 cm table seats six. Measure your room before falling in love with a table.

Are extending dining tables worth it?

Yes if you host occasionally but live in a normal-sized home. A good extending table seats four daily and six to eight at Christmas. Check the mechanism in reviews — stiff or wobbly extensions are the most common complaint.

What is the most durable dining table material?

Solid wood (oak, acacia) is the most durable and can be refinished. Tempered glass looks light but shows every fingerprint. Marble-effect MDF looks premium for less but chips more easily than real stone.

How much does a good dining set cost in the UK?

A decent 4-seater dining set costs £250–£500 in 2026; 6-seaters run £400–£900. Extending and marble-effect sets sit at the higher end. Below £200, check chair joints and table stability carefully.

Do dining chairs come assembled?

Usually not — most UK dining chairs need 10–20 minutes of assembly each, typically just legs and a few bolts. Tables often come with the top and legs separate. It's straightforward work for one person.
