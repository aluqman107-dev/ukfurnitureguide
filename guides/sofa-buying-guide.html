How to Choose a Sofa: UK Buying Guide (2026)

The complete walkthrough — measuring, sizes, fabrics, frames, delivery and care.

In short: measure your room, doorways and stair turns first; choose a hardwood frame with pocket-sprung or high-resilience foam seats; pick fabric for warmth and value or leather for durability; and budget £450–£800 for a 3-seater that lasts. The five sections below walk you through each decision.

How do I measure for a sofa?

Measuring is the step everyone skips and the one that causes most returns. Do it in this order:

  1. The room: measure wall to wall where the sofa will sit. Note radiators, plug sockets and door swings that eat into the space.
  2. The walkways: you need at least 75 cm of clear passage around the sofa — 90 cm is comfortable.
  3. The route in: measure your narrowest doorway (most UK internal doors are 76 cm), hallway width, and any staircase turns. Compare against the sofa's packaged dimensions, not just the sofa itself.
  4. The ceiling: for high-back sofas, check the height against windowsills and dado rails.

Standard UK sofa sizes (widths)

  • Loveseat / 2-seater: 120–170 cm — box rooms, snugs, flats
  • 3-seater: 180–220 cm — the average UK living room
  • Corner sofa: 230–300 cm — open-plan and large rooms
  • Seat depth: 55–60 cm suits most adults; 65 cm+ is lounge territory
  • Seat height: 43–48 cm is standard; higher suits taller or older sitters

Fabric or leather — which should I choose?

There's no universal winner; it depends on your household. Here's the honest comparison:

DurabilityGood; tightly woven lasts 7–10 yearsExcellent; 15+ years with care
CleaningStains need prompt treatmentWipes clean easily
ComfortWarm in winter, breathableCan feel cold initially, warm in summer
Kids & petsChoose stain-resistant weave; avoid light coloursTreated leather resists claws better than fabric

Our take: for most UK families, a mid-range fabric sofa with stain protection is the sweet spot — warm, affordable and replaceable. Leather earns its premium in homes where durability matters most (pets, teenagers, heavy daily use). Avoid "bonded leather" — it's plastic-coated scraps that peel within a few years.

What makes a good sofa frame?

The frame is the skeleton everything else hangs on, and it's where cheap sofas cut corners:

  • Hardwood frames (beech, ash, oak) are the gold standard — strong, stable, long-lived.
  • Softwood frames (pine) are fine at budget prices but can warp over time.
  • Chipboard / MDF frames are a red flag — they crack under stress and can't be repaired.
  • Joints should be screwed, glued and dowelled — never just stapled.
  • Seat suspension: pocket springs are best, followed by high-resilience foam (density 30 kg/m³+), then serpentine springs. Pure low-density foam sags within 2 years.

The showroom test: lift one front corner of the sofa about 15 cm. A good frame stays rigid; a poor one twists and creaks.

What should I know about sofa delivery in the UK?

UK sofa delivery: the facts

  • Timeframes: 2–4 weeks for stocked items; 8–12 weeks for made-to-order. Always get the estimate in writing.
  • Kerbside vs room-of-choice: "free delivery" often means kerbside only. Room-of-choice typically costs £20–£50 and is worth it for heavy sofas.
  • Packaging removal: usually £10–£30 extra — worth it, as sofa packaging is enormous.
  • Old sofa removal: many retailers offer it for £30–£60; councils also run bulky-waste collections.
  • Check access first: delivery drivers will refuse if it clearly won't fit — and you may still be charged.

How do I look after a sofa?

  • Plump and rotate loose cushions weekly — it evens out wear dramatically.
  • Vacuum fabric sofas monthly with an upholstery attachment (dust is abrasive).
  • Keep out of direct sun — both fabric and leather fade; rotate the sofa if one side faces a window.
  • Condition leather every 6–12 months to stop it drying and cracking.
  • Deal with spills immediately — blot, never rub. Keep a fabric cleaner matched to your upholstery type.

Sofa buying FAQs

What is the best sofa size for a UK living room?

For an average UK living room (4 × 3.5 m), a 3-seater of 190–210 cm works well. Smaller rooms suit 2-seaters (140–170 cm); large open-plan spaces can take corner sofas of 240 cm+. Always leave 75 cm of walkway around the sofa.

How do I know if a sofa is good quality?

Check three things: a hardwood frame (not just softwood or chipboard), pocket-sprung or high-resilience foam seats, and a guarantee of at least 2 years. Lift one corner — a quality sofa feels solid and doesn't twist.

Should I buy a fabric or leather sofa?

Fabric is warmer, cheaper and offers more colours; leather is harder-wearing and easier to clean but pricier. With kids or pets, choose a tightly woven fabric with stain protection, or a treated semi-aniline leather.

How long should a sofa last?

A well-made sofa lasts 7–15 years. Budget sofas (£250–£400) typically last 3–5 years before the frame or cushions give out. Rotating and plumping cushions regularly adds years of life.

What should I check about sofa delivery?

Confirm whether delivery is kerbside or room-of-choice, check the delivery timeframe (2–12 weeks is normal in the UK), measure every doorway and stair turn against the sofa's packaged dimensions, and ask about packaging removal.
