Quick buying tips

  • Measure twice. Room, doorways, hallways and stair turns — in that order. Use our sofa size finder if you're unsure.
  • Check the frame. Hardwood frames outlast softwood and engineered wood by years. It's the single biggest quality signal.
  • Sit test the seat depth. 55–60 cm suits most adults; deeper (65 cm+) is lounge territory but can be uncomfortable for shorter sitters.
  • Read the delivery terms. "Free delivery" sometimes means kerbside only. Room-of-choice delivery is worth paying for with heavy sofas.
  • Ask about the guarantee. One year is the minimum you should accept; three to five years signals a manufacturer that trusts its frame.

Sofa FAQs

What size sofa do I need for a small UK living room?

For a typical small UK living room (around 3.5 × 3 metres), a 2-seater sofa (140–170 cm wide) or a compact 3-seater (under 190 cm) works best. Leave at least 75 cm of walkway around the sofa so the room doesn't feel cramped.

Is fabric or leather better for a sofa in the UK?

Fabric is warmer, cheaper and comes in more colours, but stains more easily. Leather is hard-wearing and easy to wipe clean but costs more and can feel cold in winter. For homes with kids or pets, a tightly woven fabric or treated leather is the most practical choice.

How much should I spend on a sofa in the UK?

A decent 3-seater sofa in the UK costs £400–£800 in 2026. Budget options under £350 exist but often use weaker frames. Spending £800–£1,500 gets you better suspension, denser foam and longer guarantees.

Will a new sofa fit through my door?

Measure your narrowest doorway, hallway and staircase turn before ordering. Most UK internal doors are 76 cm wide. Many sofas come with removable legs or in two sections — check the product dimensions against your measurements, not just the room.

Do sofas come assembled in the UK?

Most UK sofas arrive in one or two pieces with only the legs or feet to attach — a ten-minute job. Corner sofas usually arrive in sections that clip together. White-glove delivery with packaging removal is often available for an extra £20–£50.
