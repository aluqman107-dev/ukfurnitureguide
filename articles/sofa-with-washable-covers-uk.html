Sofas With Washable Covers (UK 2026): What ‘Washable’ Really Means Before You Buy

Updated for 2026 · Independent advice — nobody pays for a place in this guide.

In short: a genuinely washable sofa is one whose covers — seat, back, arms and frame — all unzip and come off for the washing machine. Most sofas sold as "washable" only have removable seat cushion covers, which is a different thing entirely. Check what actually comes off before you buy, and wash at 30 degrees, never tumble dry.

Every parent, dog owner and red-wine drinker in Britain has had the same thought, usually while staring at a stain that wasn't there yesterday: why can't I just take this cover off and wash it? The answer, for most sofas, is that you can't — the upholstery is stapled, glued or stitched to the frame, and the stain is now part of the furniture. The sofas that solve this are the ones with genuinely removable covers, and they are worth seeking out, because they turn the worst sofa disasters into a wash cycle instead of a reupholstery quote.

A sofa with washable covers is a sofa whose upholstery — the seat, back and arm covers — unzips and comes off the frame so it can be washed, usually in a domestic washing machine. That definition matters, because the word "washable" on a listing is doing a lot of heavy lifting in this market. It can mean the whole sofa strips down to a bare frame. It can mean only the seat cushion covers come off. It can mean the fabric can be wiped with a damp cloth, which is not washing at all. This guide is about telling those three apart before you spend the money, because the difference between them is the difference between a sofa that survives children and one that merely survives the showroom.

What does "washable covers" actually mean on a sofa?

It means the covers unfasten from the frame — usually by zip — and come off completely, so they can be washed separately from the sofa. That is the whole test. If a listing says "washable" but only the seat cushion covers unzip, the arms and back stay dirty, and the sofa is not washable in any useful sense.

There are three levels of "washable" in the UK market, and retailers use the same word for all of them. The first is the fully removable sofa: every cover — seat cushions, back cushions, arms, base and back frame — unzips and comes off, leaving a bare frame you could, in theory, sit on. This is the real thing, and it is rarer than the marketing suggests. The second is the cushion-only sofa: the seat and back cushion covers unzip, but the frame, arms and base are fixed upholstery. This is the common one, and it is fine as far as it goes — the cushions take most of the spills — but the armrest where the dog sleeps and the side where the toddler wipes their hands stay exactly as dirty as they were. The third is the wipe-clean sofa, sold as "washable" because the fabric can be sponged: faux leather, performance fabrics and some microfibres fall into this group. Wiping is not washing, and a fabric that can be sponged is not a fabric that can go in the machine. When you read a listing, look for the words "fully removable covers" and check whether the arms and the base are included. If the listing doesn't say, ask — and if the retailer can't answer, that is your answer.

Can you machine-wash sofa covers?

Yes, if the care label says so — and only then. Fully removable cotton and polyester covers usually wash at 30 degrees on a gentle cycle, but some fabrics are hand-wash or dry-clean only, and washing the wrong cover once can shrink it past the point where it fits back on.

The care label sewn into the cover is the law of the sofa, and it beats anything the sales assistant told you. Cotton covers — the classic washable fabric, used on IKEA's Ektorp and most loose-cover designs — generally wash at 30 degrees, sometimes 40, on a gentle cycle with a mild detergent. Polyester and polyester-blend covers are more forgiving and usually wash the same way, though they can hold creases if they come out of the machine and sit in a pile. Velvet, wool blends and linen are the risky ones: many are dry-clean only, and a machine wash can shrink, felt or fade them beyond repair. The other thing the label tells you is what not to do. Koala, for example, is explicit that its classic fabric covers are hand-wash only and must not be machine washed, while its sleep-surface topper is machine washable on a cold wash. That is the kind of distinction that matters, because one wrong cycle on a £1,000 sofa is a very expensive lesson. If the label has faded or the sofa is second-hand, wash one cushion cover first — the least visible one — and see what happens before you commit the rest.

The three washable-cover tests

Before you buy, run these three checks on the listing or in the showroom. One: do the arm and base covers come off, or only the cushions? Two: does the care label say machine wash, hand wash or dry clean? Three: can you buy replacement covers separately — because a washable sofa whose covers can't be replaced is a sofa with a built-in expiry date.

Which sofas have fully removable, washable covers?

The best-known is IKEA's Ektorp, whose cotton covers unzip completely and machine-wash; Snug's Rebel and Loaf's Pudding offer washable or stain-resistant fabrics too. The pattern to look for is covers on the frame, the arms and the back — not just the seat cushions.

IKEA's Ektorp is the sofa that made washable covers famous in the UK, and it is still the benchmark: the cotton covers come off the whole frame, go in the washing machine, and replacement covers are sold separately in a range of colours, so a stained or faded cover is a £100 problem rather than a new-sofa problem. The two-seater is listed from £279, which makes it one of the cheapest genuinely washable sofas you can buy. Snug's Rebel takes a different approach: it arrives in small boxes, assembles without tools, and its covers are washable with a stain-resistant fabric underneath — built for flats where a full-size sofa can't get through the door. Loaf's Pudding is the premium end of the same idea, handmade in the UK with more than 150 fabrics, many of them washable or stain-resistant, and a deep, soft sit that makes it a genuine family sofa rather than a compromise. Koala's sofa bed range also has removable covers, but with a catch worth knowing: the outer covers zip off and wash, while the classic fabric is hand-wash only, so read the label for your colourway. The thread running through all of them is that the covers are a designed feature, not an accident — the sofa is built to be stripped down, and the retailer sells the covers separately. If a sofa claims washable covers but the retailer doesn't sell replacements, be suspicious: it usually means the covers were never meant to come off regularly.

What happens to sofa covers after washing?

Mostly, nothing bad — cotton covers washed at 30 degrees and dried on the line come out clean and fit again. The damage comes from heat: tumble drying and hot washes shrink cotton, fade colours and can warp the fit, which is why manufacturers say to reshape covers while wet and dry them flat or on the line.

The first wash is the one people worry about, and mostly they needn't. A cotton cover washed at 30 degrees on a gentle cycle, then reshaped while damp and dried on the line, comes out clean and goes back on the frame the way it came off. The problems start with heat. A hot wash — 60 degrees because the stain looked serious — shrinks cotton by a few percent, which is enough to make a tight cover impossible to zip back on. A tumble dryer does the same damage faster, and it fades colours at the same time. Sunlight is the quieter culprit: a cover dried in strong sun bleaches unevenly, so the seat cushion fades faster than the back cushion, and the sofa ends up two shades of the same colour. The fix is boring and it works: wash cool, reshape the cover while it is still wet — pull it into shape on the cushion before it dries — and dry it on the line in the shade or flat indoors. Dark fabrics deserve a colour-safe detergent and a wash with similar colours, and new covers should be washed once before they go on the sofa, because the first wash takes out the factory stiffness and any loose dye. And keep the zips done up in the wash, or the teeth catch on everything else in the drum.

Are washable covers worth it with kids and pets?

Yes — for households with children, dogs or cats, washable covers are the single most practical sofa feature there is. A spill, a muddy paw or a sick child stops being a permanent stain and becomes a wash cycle, which is why family sofas with removable covers outlast fixed upholstery by years.

This is where the feature earns its money. A fixed sofa in a house with a toddler has a lifespan measured in stains: the juice, the chocolate, the felt-tip pen, the sick — each one a small disaster that spot-cleaning only half fixes. A washable sofa turns each one into a forty-minute wash cycle, and the sofa looks the same afterwards. Dogs are the same story in a different shape: muddy paws, wet fur and the smell that builds up in the fabric over months. A washable cover goes in the machine and the smell goes with it, which no amount of fabric spray achieves on fixed upholstery. Cats are the one exception worth naming, because washable covers don't solve scratching — a cat's claws will hook a loose-weave cotton cover the same way they hook any fabric, and for that you need a tight weave, which our cat-proofing guide covers in full. The other thing washable covers buy you is time: a sofa whose covers can be washed and replaced lasts years longer than one whose fabric is fixed, because the frame outlives the fabric and the fabric is the part you can now renew. How do I wash sofa covers without ruining them?

Check the care label first, unzip every cover, and wash at 30 degrees on a gentle cycle with a mild detergent. Put covers back on while they are still slightly damp, so they dry to the shape of the cushions, and never tumble dry them unless the label explicitly allows it.

The routine is simple once you know it, and the order matters. First, vacuum the covers before they come off — loose crumbs and pet hair in the washing machine end up in the filter and on everything else. Second, zip every cover closed before it goes in the drum, and wash covers from the same sofa together so they fade evenly. Third, use a mild liquid detergent, not a powder, and skip the fabric softener: it coats the fibres, reduces stain resistance and builds up over repeated washes. Fourth, wash at 30 degrees on a gentle or delicates cycle, and don't overload the machine — covers need room to move, and a crammed drum creases them into shapes they keep. Fifth, and most important, put the covers back on the cushions while they are still slightly damp. A damp cover stretches onto the cushion and dries to its shape; a fully dry cover is stiff, shrunken and a fight to zip. If a cover has shrunk slightly, a light steam — a handheld steamer, or the steam setting on an iron held a few centimetres away — will usually relax it enough to fit. And one last rule from the manufacturers: reshape covers while wet and dry them flat or on the line in the shade, with no tumble drying and no ironing unless the label says otherwise. Follow that, and the covers will outlast the cushions underneath them.

Sofas with washable covers FAQs

Are all sofa covers washable?

No. Only covers that unzip and come off the frame are washable, and even then the care label decides: some are machine washable, some are hand-wash only, and some are dry-clean only. A sofa whose covers are fixed to the frame cannot be washed at all, whatever the listing says.

Can I put sofa covers in the washing machine?

Yes, if the care label says machine wash — most removable cotton and polyester covers wash at 30 degrees on a gentle cycle. Zip the covers closed first, use a mild liquid detergent, skip fabric softener, and put them back on the cushions while still slightly damp.

Do washable sofa covers shrink?

They can, if washed hot or tumble dried — cotton shrinks with heat. Wash at 30 degrees, never tumble dry, and reshape the cover while it is still wet so it dries to the shape of the cushion. A slight shrink can usually be relaxed with steam.

Which fabric is best for washable sofa covers?

Tightly woven cotton and polyester blends: they wash well, dry fast and hold their shape. Avoid loose weaves and velvet for washable covers — they shrink, fade and lose their texture in the machine, and many are dry-clean only anyway.

How often should I wash sofa covers?

Seat cushion covers every two to three months in a normal household, more often with children or pets — and immediately after any spill. The frame and arm covers can go longer, but wash the whole set together when you do, so the colours fade evenly.
