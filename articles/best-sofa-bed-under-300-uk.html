Best Sofa Beds Under £300 (UK 2026)

Updated for 2026 · Independent advice — nobody pays for a place in this guide.

In short: most sofa-bed buying guides review them as sofas. This one reviews them as beds — because a sofa bed nobody can sleep on is just an expensive sofa. Under £300, click-clack mechanisms offer the best sleep quality for the money, pull-outs need careful mattress-thickness checks, and futon-style are fine for occasional use only. Judge every option by mattress thickness first, mechanism second, sofa looks third.

The average guest stays two nights. On a bad sofa bed, that's two nights of bar-in-the-back misery that your guest will remember far longer than your hospitality. Retailers love selling sofa beds on sofa criteria — colour, style, how it looks folded — because most cheap sofa beds fail on bed criteria. This guide flips the priority: sleep first, sofa second.

The mattress-thickness rule

The single most predictive spec: how thick is the sleeping surface?

  • Under 10 cm: occasional emergency use only. Fine for the nephew who crashes once a year; cruel for your mother-in-law's week-long visit.
  • 10–14 cm: the honest budget zone. Comfortable for 2–4 night stays for average-weight sleepers. This is where the decent sub-£300 options live.
  • 14 cm+: genuinely good sleep. Rare under £300 except in click-clacks using the full seat cushions.

If a listing won't state the sleeping thickness, assume the worst. Retailers proud of their sleep surface put the number front and centre; retailers hiding it know exactly why.

The bar test

Pull-out mechanisms have a support bar across the middle that you feel through thin mattresses — the classic sofa-bed complaint. Under £300, check reviews specifically for "bar" mentions. Click-clacks avoid this entirely (no bar), which is a major reason they're the budget sweet spot.

Click-clack: the budget winner

Click-clack (or "clin-click") sofa beds convert by folding the backrest down flat, creating a bed from the seat and back cushions. No separate mattress, no bars, no gaps — just a flat sleeping surface 10–14 cm thick. Conversion takes seconds, and the mechanism is simple enough that there's little to break.

The honest limitations: the sleeping surface is only as good as the seat cushions, so cheap click-clacks with thin foam cushions sleep thin. And the unfolded bed is wide but not long — check the length suits your tallest guest (most are 180–190 cm, fine for average adults). Styling is functional rather than beautiful, though velvet-covered versions look far better than the name suggests.

Pull-out: check before you buy

Pull-outs look more like "real" sofas when folded, which is their appeal — but the mechanism introduces the two failure modes above: the centre gap where sections meet, and the support bar felt through thin mattresses. Under £300, pull-outs vary enormously: the good ones (thicker mattress, well-designed mechanism) sleep well; the bad ones are decorative.

Non-negotiable checks: mattress thickness stated (12 cm+ preferred), reviews mentioning the bar (or its absence), and the unfolded length. Also check the daily-sofa comfort — pull-outs used as the main living-room sofa need to be good sofas too, and some prioritise the bed at the sofa's expense.

Futon-style: honest about limits

Traditional futon sofa beds (back folds flat) are the cheapest option and perfectly honest about it: fine for occasional guests, students, and kids' sleepovers. Don't buy one expecting it to serve as a regular guest bed for adults — the thin sleeping surface and low height wear thin quickly, literally and figuratively.

The guest-room reality checklist

  • Bedding storage: where do the duvet and pillows live when it's a sofa? If the answer is "the hallway cupboard is full", factor in an ottoman or storage box.
  • Unfolded footprint: a double sofa bed needs ~140 × 190 cm clear floor when open, plus walking space. Tape it out.
  • Who sleeps there: elderly guests struggle with low futon heights and thin surfaces — a higher click-clack or proper pull-out is kinder.
  • How often: monthly+ guests deserve the best mechanism you can afford; twice-a-year guests don't justify stretching the budget.

Sofa bed vs the alternatives

Before spending £250, consider whether a sofa bed is even the right answer:

  • Folding guest bed (£80–£150): a proper folding bed with a real mattress folds to ~30 cm deep and leans against a wall. Sleeps better than most sub-£300 sofa beds, costs half as much — but it's undeniably a bed in your living room when unfolded, and an object to store when folded. Best when guests are frequent and the "sofa" function isn't needed.
  • Air mattress (£40–£100): modern raised air beds with built-in pumps are surprisingly comfortable for a weekend and pack into a bag. But they leak (slowly, always), need power, and feel temporary — fine for the nephew, insulting for the in-laws.
  • Day bed (£150–£300): the elegant compromise — a proper single bed that looks like a sofa by day. Sleeps one brilliantly, but only one, and it permanently occupies single-bed floor space.

The sofa bed wins when the room must function as a living room daily and sleep two occasionally. If either condition is false — guests sleep elsewhere, or the room is really a bedroom — the alternatives are cheaper and often more comfortable.

Don't forget the sofa half

This guide weights the bed experience because retailers don't — but you'll sit on it far more than you'll sleep on it. A sofa bed that's miserable as a sofa is a failed purchase whatever the mattress.

Check seat depth (52–58 cm suits most adults), back height (low backs look sleek and support nothing), and arm comfort. Sit-test in a showroom if possible; if buying online, read the sofa reviews, not just the bed reviews. The ideal sub-£300 sofa bed is a good £250 sofa that happens to unfold — not a £250 bed pretending to be a sofa.

One specific trap: pull-out mechanisms often make the seat unusually deep or high (the mechanism lives underneath). If you're short, sit in it — feet dangling off a 48 cm seat height gets old fast.

Sofa bed FAQs

Can you sleep on a sofa bed every night?

A quality sofa bed (£400+) with a proper mattress, yes — many people do. Under £300, treat it as excellent for guests and tolerable short-term, but not as a permanent bed. Nightly use will also wear the mechanism faster than occasional use.

What's the most comfortable type of sofa bed?

Judged purely on sleep: click-clack with thick cushions, because the surface is flat with no bars or gaps. Pull-outs with thick mattresses run close second. The mechanism matters less than the thickness and quality of what you lie on.

How do I make a sofa bed more comfortable for guests?

A 5 cm memory foam topper (£40–£60) transforms most sofa beds — it bridges gaps, covers bars, and adds the thickness cheap mechanisms lack. See our topper guide. Plus proper pillows; guests forgive a firm bed faster than a flat pillow.

Do sofa beds fit through standard doorways?

Usually yes — most are designed for it, and many have removable arms or legs. Measure the narrowest point of your delivery route anyway; sofa beds are bulky and awkward rather than heavy.

Click-clack vs pull-out — which lasts longer?

Click-clacks, generally — fewer moving parts, nothing to jam. Pull-out mechanisms have more to go wrong, though quality ones last years. At identical prices, the click-clack is the lower-risk mechanism.
