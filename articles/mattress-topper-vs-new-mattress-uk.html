Mattress Topper vs New Mattress: Which Should You Buy? (UK 2026)

Updated for 2026 · Independent advice — nobody pays for a place in this guide.

In short: a mattress topper (£40–£120) is the right fix when your mattress is structurally sound but too firm, too cold, or past its comfortable prime. A new mattress (£150–£600+) is the right fix when yours sags, dips, or wakes you with aches. The topper can't fix a broken mattress — but it can postpone a £400 purchase by years. Below: how to tell which camp you're in.

It's the most economical question in bedding: your mattress isn't quite right anymore, and you're staring at a £400 replacement versus a £70 topper. Retailers, unsurprisingly, would prefer you buy the mattress. But sometimes the topper genuinely is the smarter buy — and sometimes it's £70 down the drain. The difference is knowing what each one can and can't fix.

Scope note: this covers comfort and practical differences, not medical advice. Persistent pain deserves a GP or physiotherapist, not a shopping guide.

What a topper can fix

  • Too-firm mattress. The classic topper win. A 5–7 cm memory foam or latex topper adds the pressure relief a firm mattress lacks — effectively changing the comfort layer without replacing the support underneath.
  • A mattress that's "fine but tired". 5–7 years old, still flat, just less comfortable than it was. A topper refreshes the sleep surface for a fraction of replacement cost.
  • Temperature. Wool toppers add breathable warmth in winter; some cooling toppers help hot sleepers on foam mattresses. (Honest caveat: a topper can't make a heat-trapping foam mattress breathe like springs.)
  • Guest beds and rentals. Transforming a rock-hard spare mattress or a landlord's basic offering without spending mattress money.
  • Protecting a new mattress. A topper takes the wear — body oils, compression — extending the mattress underneath. Cheaper to replace a £70 topper than a £400 mattress.

What a topper can not fix

  • Sagging or body impressions. A topper follows the shape beneath it. Put 5 cm of foam over a 4 cm dip and you have a slightly softer dip. This is the number one topper disappointment, and it's entirely predictable.
  • Broken-down support. If springs are poking, sides are collapsing, or you roll into the middle, the support layer has failed. No comfort layer fixes failed support.
  • Allergies to the old mattress. A topper doesn't seal in dust mites effectively enough to matter — and old mattresses are mite cities. Replace, don't cover.
  • Wrong size or type. A topper can't make a too-small mattress bigger or a sprung mattress feel like foam (well, a memory foam topper gets partway there).

The 2-minute sag test

Strip the bed and lay a straight edge (a broom handle works) across the mattress. If you can slide more than two fingers underneath at the dip, the mattress has sagged beyond what any topper can fix. Time for a new one — see our best mattresses guide.

The value maths: a £70 topper that buys your £400 mattress three more good years costs £23/year. Even if you eventually replace the mattress, the topper wasn't wasted — it bought time and better sleep meanwhile. But a £70 topper on a dead mattress is £70 wasted plus the £400 you spend anyway.

Topper types: which material?

  • Memory foam (5–7 cm): the most popular for a reason — genuine pressure relief, good motion isolation. Sleeps warm; look for open-cell or gel-infused if heat bothers you. Density matters: 50kg/m³+ lasts; cheap thin toppers flatten within a year.
  • Latex (5 cm): responsive like springs, contouring like foam, sleeps cooler than memory foam. Costs more (£80–£150) but outlasts foam toppers significantly. The connoisseur's choice.
  • Feather/down alternative (5–10 cm): plush hotel-bed feel, but compresses quickly and does almost nothing for support. Luxury feel, not a firmness fix.
  • Wool: temperature-regulating and naturally resistant to dust mites. Lovely, but expensive (£100+) and needs airing. Best as a comfort/temperature layer rather than a firmness fix.

Thickness rule: under 5 cm is a mattress protector with marketing; 5–7 cm actually changes feel; over 8 cm starts causing fitted-sheet problems (you'll need deep/extra-deep sheets).

When the answer is definitely "new mattress"

  • Visible sagging or body impressions deeper than ~4 cm
  • You wake with aches that fade within an hour of getting up (classic dead-mattress symptom)
  • The mattress is 10+ years old (even without visible sag, materials degrade)
  • Springs you can feel, lumps, or structural noises
  • Your needs changed: partner, weight change, injury recovery — a topper can't re-engineer support

The smart sequence

If you're unsure, there's a rational order of operations:

  1. Do the sag test above. Failed → new mattress. Passed → continue.
  2. Identify the complaint. Too firm / too cold / just tired → topper territory. Sagging / pain / ancient → new mattress.
  3. Try the topper first if eligible. £70 risk versus £400 commitment. Give it three weeks.
  4. If the topper doesn't fix it in a month, it won't. Stop optimising and buy the mattress — the topper becomes a guest-bed upgrade, not a waste.

Toppers for specific situations

Renters: toppers are the renter's secret weapon. You can't replace the landlord's mattress, but a 7 cm memory foam topper transforms it — and rolls up to take with you when you move. It's the highest comfort-per-pound upgrade available to anyone who doesn't own their bed.

Couples who disagree: one likes firm, one likes soft? A topper on one side only (single toppers exist, or fold a double) creates a split-firmness bed for £50. It's not elegant, but it ends the argument — and it's far cheaper than the split mattresses sold for this purpose.

New mattress too firm: surprisingly common — people buy "medium-firm" and find it board-like for the first month. Before returning it, try a topper: new mattresses soften slightly with use, and a topper bridges the break-in period. Many "too firm" returns could have been £60 topper fixes.

Back pain sufferers: not medical advice — but the pattern we see in reviews is consistent: toppers help when the issue is pressure points on a too-firm surface, and do nothing when the issue is sagging support. If a topper makes things worse within two weeks, stop and see a professional rather than persevering.

Topper care: making it last

Toppers die faster than mattresses because they're thinner and take direct body contact. Three habits extend life meaningfully:

  • Rotate monthly. Head-to-foot rotation evens out compression — the single highest-impact habit, taking thirty seconds.
  • Air it. Strip the bed weekly and let the topper breathe for an hour. Moisture trapped in foam breaks it down; airflow is free life extension.
  • Wash the cover, not the topper. Most toppers have removable covers — wash those. Never machine-wash a memory foam topper itself; you'll destroy its structure. Spot-clean only.

When the topper develops its own body impressions that don't recover overnight, it's finished. Don't stack a new topper on a dead topper — that's how you build a very expensive, very lumpy mattress.

Topper vs new mattress FAQs

Can a mattress topper fix a sagging mattress?

No — and this is the most important answer on the page. A topper conforms to the shape beneath it, so it softens a dip without removing it. Sagging deeper than ~4 cm needs a new mattress.

How thick should a mattress topper be?

5–7 cm to actually change the feel. Under 5 cm is padding, not transformation. Over 8 cm causes fitted-sheet problems unless you buy deep sheets.

Do toppers make you sleep hot?

Memory foam toppers add warmth — the same heat-trapping physics as foam mattresses, just thinner. Hot sleepers should choose latex or wool toppers, or address the mattress itself.

How long do mattress toppers last?

2–4 years for memory foam (density-dependent), 4–6 for latex, 1–2 for feather. When the topper itself develops body impressions, it's done — toppers don't get second chances.

Is it worth putting an expensive topper on a cheap mattress?

Up to a point. A £100 latex topper on a structurally sound £150 mattress is smart economics. A £100 topper on a dead mattress is lipstick on a pig — do the sag test first.
