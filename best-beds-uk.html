Quick buying tips

  • Match the mattress size exactly. UK sizes are standardised — single 90×190, double 135×190, king 150×200, super king 180×200 cm.
  • Inspect the slats. Sprung slats flex and suit most mattresses; solid slats need gaps under 7 cm to support foam mattresses properly.
  • Measure the room, not just the bed. Allow 60 cm each side for getting in and out, and check the headboard height against windowsills.
  • Storage beds need clearance. Ottoman beds need room to lift; drawer divans need 60 cm of pull-out space.
  • Spend on the mattress first. A £200 frame with a £500 mattress sleeps better than the reverse.

Bed FAQs

What are standard UK bed sizes?

UK bed sizes are: single 90×190 cm, small double 120×190 cm, double 135×190 cm, king 150×200 cm, super king 180×200 cm. Always buy the mattress in the same size as the frame.

Is an ottoman bed worth it?

If you're short on storage, yes. An ottoman bed gives you the entire under-bed area as storage — roughly four large suitcases' worth in a double. The gas-lift mechanism should feel smooth and stay open safely on its own.

Divan or bed frame — which is better?

Divans are practical: compact footprint, often with drawers, and easy to move. Bed frames (wooden or upholstered) look better and suit larger rooms. For small UK box rooms, a divan usually wins on practicality.

How much should a good bed cost in the UK?

A solid double bed frame costs £250–£600 in 2026; ottoman and TV beds run £500–£1,200. Below £200, check the slat quality and frame joints carefully — that's where cheap beds fail.

Do I need a new mattress with a new bed?

Not necessarily, but check the fit: UK mattresses and frames are standardised, so your old mattress fits if it's the same size. If your mattress is over 8 years old or sagging, replace both together for the best sleep.
